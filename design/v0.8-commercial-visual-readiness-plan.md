# The Lantern Line v0.8 Commercial Visual Readiness Plan

Date: September 23, 2026

Status: Binding commercial-presentation companion to
`design/v0.8-visual-mobile-overhaul-plan.md`

Baseline: approximately 3/10 commercial visual readiness, as recorded in
`design/audits/v0.8-complete-visual-profitability-audit.md`

Target: at least 8/10 commercial visual readiness before any paid-launch
recommendation or public v0.8 replacement of the current v0.7 build

## Executive decision

The existing v0.8 architecture remains correct:

- Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility/WebGL 2 remains the shipping renderer.
- Layered Blender-authored sprite rigs remain the default gameplay path.
- Selective half-resolution live 3D remains limited to approved High-profile
  landmarks, short transitions, and Longshadow presentation.
- Medium, Low, touch-auto, and vector fallback remain fully supported.
- Gameplay, saves, timing, deterministic outcomes, and accessibility contracts
  remain unchanged.

The production sequence changes in one important way:

> M2 is completed first, then one final-quality commercial proof slice must
> score at least 8/10 in every represented critical category before the
> remaining art is scaled across the full game.

This avoids producing six cars, twelve upgrades, three threats, multiple world
kits, and a boss with an art language that has not yet proved it can create a
desirable screenshot, a strong mobile frame, or a convincing gameplay trailer.

This plan is designed to be executed autonomously through reproducible Blender,
Godot, Python, browser-capture, and audit tooling. It does not require an
external artist or a GUI-only production step. Physical or hosted-device
access, device-farm credentials, store account actions, and real-player market
testing remain the only unavoidable activities that cannot be manufactured by
the asset pipeline itself.

## What this plan can and cannot promise

An 8/10 score in this plan means:

- the game looks intentionally authored rather than provisional
- its hero train, threats, upgrades, effects, and boss read clearly at desktop
  and phone size
- its strongest actions produce store-quality stills and short loops
- its visual identity is distinguishable from the increasingly crowded
  train-defense field
- its presentation survives accessibility and low-profile fallbacks
- its marketing assets honestly represent the game
- its Web build remains inside measured performance and package budgets

It does not guarantee profitability. Revenue also depends on pricing,
wishlists, audience fit, launch timing, retention, reviews, competition, and
store visibility. The plan improves the product's ability to convert attention
into interest; it does not fabricate market demand.

## Research conclusions

### 1. The train-defense niche is no longer visually empty

By September 2026, Steam contains or is promoting multiple train-centered
tower-defense, roguelite, survival, and moving-base games, including:

- All Aboard! The Train Defense Express
- Bullet Train
- Choo Choo Defenders
- Railhold
- Railwarden
- Train Towers
- Train Trek
- Dwarven Rails

The Lantern Line therefore cannot rely on "tower defense on a train" as its
complete visual pitch. Generic bronze steampunk, large turret counts, top-down
track building, and upgrade clutter already occupy that space.

The visual pitch must instead be:

> A fortress train crosses an impossible night. The headlight is a weapon,
> every car is a life, and surviving may mean cutting one loose.

The commercially differentiating images are the side-view nocturnal
composition, the controlled headlight cone, lens-specific supernatural
threats, a visibly inhabited train, sacrifice through real-car detachment, and
the Longshadow consuming the rail horizon.

### 2. Successful references do not share one rendering style

The research set includes Thronefall, Bad North, Dome Keeper, Kingdom Rush 5:
Alliance, Bloons TD 6, Monster Train 2, and The Last Spell. Their fidelity and
rendering techniques differ substantially.

Their shared commercial traits are:

- one-frame silhouette recognition
- one coherent material, lighting, and color language
- clearly authored idle, locomotion, attack, reaction, and death states
- visible ownership and progression
- strong anticipation before contact
- material-specific response after impact
- a small number of memorable actions suitable for screenshots and trailers
- interfaces that visually belong to the world
- marketing assets made from real gameplay rather than concept promises

The Lantern Line should not imitate the content density of Kingdom Rush or the
illustration volume of Monster Train 2. It should use the production economy of
Thronefall and Bad North: a compact vocabulary executed consistently, with
more animation attention concentrated on the train, Focus, detachment, and
Longshadow.

### 3. Storefront quality is part of the visual product

Valve's current Steamworks guidance makes several requirements directly
relevant to production:

- players may give a trailer less than ten seconds
- the first trailer should primarily show gameplay
- visible in-game HUD can help communicate how the game is played
- store screenshots should be actual gameplay
- at least five screenshots are required
- screenshots should be at least 1920 x 1080 and 16:9
- the 462 x 174 small capsule is automatically reduced to 120 x 45 and
  184 x 69
- base capsules may contain game artwork, the title, and an official subtitle,
  but not review scores, awards, or promotional copy

Store captures, capsule compositions, and the first ten seconds of the trailer
must therefore be developed during the proof slice, not after the game is
already visually complete.

### 4. Web and mobile reward authored assets, not uncontrolled effects

Godot 4 Web exports use WebGL 2.0 and the Compatibility renderer. Forward+,
Mobile, and compositor effects are not available on Web. Mobile Web also has
less CPU and GPU headroom than native mobile.

The resulting art strategy is:

- bake form, broad lighting, ambient occlusion, and material identity into the
  authored assets
- use CanvasTexture normal/specular data only where it is visibly useful
- reserve dynamic Light2D use for the headlight, cabin/lantern response, and
  selected signature moments
- use pooled sprite sequences and preconfigured one-shot emitters for critical
  effects
- do not depend on `GPUParticles2D.emit_particle()`, which is unavailable in
  the Compatibility renderer
- use SubViewport update-once behavior for static scenes and continuous
  updates only during short approved live-3D sequences
- preserve the accepted 2048 x 2048 atlas limit and existing texture,
  particle, memory, package, and draw-call gates

### 5. Accessibility variants must be authored with the effect

Reduced Motion, Reduced Flashes, High Contrast, text scaling, explicit labels,
and screen-shake controls are already product strengths.

The new presentation must preserve them by design:

- no effect may flash more than three times in one second
- default full-screen luminance changes remain rare and bounded
- Reduced Flashes replaces bright pulses with outlines, shape changes,
  sustained markers, and localized value shifts
- Reduced Motion removes camera shake, parallax, nonessential sway, bobbing,
  and drifting while retaining attack timing
- critical boundaries target at least 3:1 local contrast in default
  presentation and 4.5:1 in High Contrast
- no critical state may be communicated only by motion, depth, color, flash,
  or sound

## Commercial visual identity

### North-star statement

Every production decision should strengthen this image:

> A heavy, inhabited machine pushes a cone of warm safety through an endless
> supernatural night while distinct shapes gather at its edges.

### Six visual pillars

#### 1. The headlight shapes the world

The beam is not an overlay. It reveals material, exposes threat anatomy, casts
the train's visual hierarchy, and creates the strongest warm/cold contrast in
the frame.

Required reads:

- broad travel beam
- lens change
- Focus compression
- threat exposure
- ward fracture
- exhausted recovery

#### 2. The train is the hero character

The locomotive receives the highest asset, rig, animation, and lighting budget.
Cars are not repeated rectangles; they are organs of one connected machine.

Required reads:

- mass and suspension
- powered and unpowered state
- acceleration and braking
- working mechanisms
- weapon recoil
- damage and repair
- crew activity
- coupling tension
- detachment recoil and aftermath

#### 3. Progression changes silhouette

Every purchased car and upgrade must be visible without reading a label.
Recolors alone do not count.

The six base cars and twelve upgrade branches must produce recognizably
different rooflines, side profiles, mounted machinery, window rhythms, or
rear silhouettes.

#### 4. Threat behavior is readable from motion

The Pursuer, Boarder, and Drainer must be identifiable by center of mass,
locomotion line, and attack preparation before their labels appear.

#### 5. Effects describe material and consequence

Effects exist to show what hit what, from which direction, with what force, and
what changed afterward. They do not exist to fill the screen.

#### 6. The UI is a conductor's instrument panel

The interface should use brass rails, gauges, shutter shapes, route signals,
etched labels, and mechanical cooldown movement while preserving the strong
information hierarchy and touch geometry already implemented.

## Signature commercial moments

The complete game must produce twelve authored six-second presentation loops:

1. Headlight reveal of a Pursuer.
2. Standard Defense Platform firing and impact.
3. Heavy Cannon recoil and heavy impact.
4. Flak tracking, burst, and aerial reaction.
5. Focus charge, compression, contact, and recovery.
6. Shadow Ward fracture and shatter.
7. Workshop repair and visible train recovery.
8. Real-car coupling break, recoil, separation, and aftermath.
9. Longshadow Veil transformation.
10. Longshadow Tether transformation.
11. Longshadow Charge transformation.
12. Dawn Beacon arrival and release.

Each loop must contain:

1. a stable starting state
2. readable anticipation
3. contact or transformation
4. a victim, machine, or environment reaction
5. recovery or a persistent consequence

Sound improves these loops but may not be required to understand them.

## The 8/10 scoring contract

### Score anchors

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0-2 | Missing, broken, or purely placeholder |
| 3-4 | Functional prototype presentation |
| 5-6 | Coherent but visibly limited indie production |
| 7 | Credible paid-game quality with notable weaknesses |
| 8 | Commercially convincing within the chosen scope |
| 9 | Distinctive, highly polished, and memorable |
| 10 | Genre-leading execution |

The production target is **8.3**, not exactly 8.0. The margin protects against
reviewer variance and late integration loss.

Release readiness requires:

- weighted score at least 8.0
- internal production target at least 8.3
- every category at least 7.0
- every critical category at least 8.0
- no unresolved high-confidence launch blocker

Critical categories are marked below.

### Weighted scorecard

| Category | Weight | 8/10 evidence | Floor |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| **Market distinctiveness and art direction** | 12 | A frame reads as The Lantern Line rather than generic steampunk; sprite, UI, VFX, and live 3D share Coal-lit Ink & Brass | **8** |
| **Hero train and upgrade ownership** | 12 | The locomotive has premium finish; all six cars and twelve upgrades are recognizable from silhouette and state | **8** |
| **Animation and mechanical weight** | 12 | Train, crew, weapons, couplings, and damage use authored states with secondary motion and convincing recovery | **8** |
| **Threat and boss personality** | 10 | Each threat reads by shape and motion; Longshadow phases read without text | **8** |
| **Combat VFX and material response** | 10 | Every signature action has anticipation, contact, reaction, aftermath, and an accessible variant | **8** |
| Environment depth and composition | 8 | Four depth bands, route identity, landmark variety, and focal control survive compact views | 7 |
| UI visual integration and feedback | 8 | Panels, icons, cooldowns, focus, warnings, and transitions belong to the train fantasy without reducing clarity | 7 |
| **Mobile and accessibility presentation** | 10 | 844 x 390 remains desirable and readable at all target scales; authored High Contrast, Reduced Motion, and Reduced Flashes pass | **8** |
| Performance, reproducibility, and fallback | 8 | Existing frame, draw, package, profile, provenance, and deterministic gates pass | 7 |
| **Storefront and trailer readiness** | 10 | Capsules survive final downscales; real gameplay yields strong screenshots and a silent-readable first ten seconds | **8** |

Weights total 100.

### Evidence and review method

Every score is supported by artifacts, not a prose assertion.

Required evidence:

- 1280 x 720, 960 x 540, and 844 x 390 captures
- default, High Contrast, Reduced Motion, and Reduced Flashes variants
- neutral, silhouette, material, normal/mask, extreme-pose, and animation
  contact sheets
- six-second loops for all signature actions present in the milestone
- frame-time, draw-call, memory, and package reports
- source and generated-asset provenance
- small-capsule 120 x 45 and 184 x 69 reductions
- muted ten-second gameplay opening
- vector fallback comparison

Reviewers:

1. implementation audit with full technical context
2. independent visual critic receiving only captures and loops
3. commercial-readiness critic receiving captures, capsules, and trailer but
   not implementation notes

Use the median category score. A critical-category failure from two reviewers
blocks the milestone. A reviewer spread greater than 1.5 points triggers a
specific ambiguity audit instead of averaging the disagreement away.

For an intermediate milestone, re-normalize only the weights of categories
represented by production-ready content in that milestone. A category marked
not applicable contributes neither points nor weight. It may not be marked not
applicable once its named production scope has begun. In M3B, the critical
threat-and-boss category scores the Pursuer evidence only; Longshadow becomes
mandatory and receives the complete category score in M6.

This is an autonomous internal gate, not a substitute for unfamiliar human
players.

## Objective image and motion tests

### Silhouette test

- convert subject and upgrade sheets to one-color silhouettes
- view them at the actual in-game size and at 50% scale
- the locomotive must remain the dominant horizontal mass
- all six car families must be distinguishable from roofline, side mass, or
  attachment rhythm
- at least five of six base/upgrade comparisons must be correctly identified
  without labels by each independent critic

### No-text test

Hide tutorial banners and nonessential labels.

The reviewer must still identify:

- train power and damage state
- threat family
- attack preparation
- impact direction
- whether the action succeeded
- whether a car was lost
- Longshadow phase

### Mobile composition test

At 844 x 390:

- clear-world height remains at or above the accepted M1 floor
- use 12% of physical viewport height for the train and 7% for a regular threat
  as initial M3A calibration targets, derived within the accepted M1 clear-world
  region
- primary contact effects occupy enough area to survive phone scaling
- controls never cover the active contact point
- the frame remains attractive at 100%, 115%, and 130% target scale

M3A records the accepted physical-pixel and percentage values from the chosen
style treatment. Those measured values become the release gate. They may be
loosened only with side-by-side evidence showing equal or better readability.

If these requirements conflict, composition, camera crop, world scale, or
asset scale changes before control targets are reduced.

### Thumbnail test

At 320 pixels wide:

- one focal action remains obvious
- the locomotive remains recognizable
- foreground effects do not merge into noise
- the screenshot has one readable story

### Capsule test

At 120 x 45:

- the title remains legible
- one hero silhouette or headlight shape remains visible
- no secondary detail competes with the logo
- the image is not mistaken for a generic top-down train game

### Motion test

For each signature action:

- anticipation is visible before contact
- contact is not hidden by a full-screen flash
- attacker and victim both react where appropriate
- recovery does not snap to idle
- Reduced Motion preserves timing through shape, outline, opacity, or held
  poses
- the simulation timeline remains untouched

1. iron and steel
2. heat, ember, and smoke

- localized light/value response

- `world`
- `effects`

### Style master

Create one controlled style source containing:

- gameplay orthographic camera
- approved camera pitch and framing
- coal key light
- ember rim light
- controlled fill
- semantic material library
- outline/ink treatment
- color management
- render resolution presets
- canonical world ground and scale references

Every subject imports or links the same approved rig. Subject files may not
invent their own light or color pipeline.

### Blender output passes

Generate only passes that create visible value:

- combined/diffuse color
- alpha
- normal
- ambient occlusion or shadow
- emission
- material mask
- object/part masks where modular assembly requires them

Normal/specular data is mandatory for the locomotive, selected train
mechanisms, important landmarks, and Longshadow surfaces that visibly benefit.
It is optional for small threats and distant world layers if the mobile capture
shows no meaningful improvement.

### Deterministic build

The asset build must:

- run Blender 4.5.14 LTS in background mode
- use fixed seeds, samples, camera, lights, color settings, and output names
- generate High and Low variants with identical pivots and sockets
- pack no atlas larger than 2048 x 2048
- emit Godot resources and metadata
- record source, tool, artifact, topology, rig, action, and atlas hashes
- rebuild twice during the stage audit
- compare exact hashes where stable and perceptual/structural evidence where
  renderer output is hardware-sensitive

Canonical references continue to use the existing CPU Cycles verification
policy. Local GPU renders may accelerate iteration but do not replace the
canonical pass.

### Atlas policy

- never bake the entire train as one full-frame animation
- static bodies bake once
- wheels, rods, turrets, fans, doors, gauges, shutters, couplings, and tools
  remain separate parts
- flipbooks are reserved for deformation, cloth, smoke, supernatural matter,
  and effects
- High and Low atlases share metadata
- normal/material atlases unload when the selected profile does not use them

## Runtime animation grammar

### Ownership

- `AnimationPlayer` owns reusable authored clips
- `AnimationTree` owns blended crew and Longshadow states
- `AnimatedSprite2D` owns compact threat deformation and critical effect
  flipbooks
- transforms own rigid mechanical motion
- tweens own target-dependent cosmetic destinations
- pooled view scenes own all repeated threats and effects

Animation never emits authoritative gameplay outcomes. It consumes snapshots
and presentation events from M2.

### Four-beat action rule

Every attack or major response contains:

1. anticipation
2. contact
3. reaction
4. recovery

The existing presentation-bible timing bands remain authoritative:

- threat anticipation: 0.35-0.8 seconds
- impact impulse: 0.08-0.16 seconds
- heavy train reaction: 0.25-0.55 seconds
- boss flourish: 0.7-1.2 seconds inside protected timing

### Deterministic-safe impact

Do not change `Engine.time_scale` and do not pause simulation for hitstop.

Allowed cosmetic responses:

- local sprite scale or shear punch
- short visual-only animation-speed reduction
- bounded camera trauma
- directional visual offset
- localized exposure or outline response
- orthographic zoom pulse within the screen-shake setting
- persistent damage or debris state

Reduced Motion disables camera and zoom impulses and uses held poses,
outlines, value shifts, and localized state changes instead.

### Train motion system

Required channels:

- wheel speed tied to train speed
- rod phase tied to wheel rotation
- locomotive suspension
- phase-offset car suspension
- coupling compression and extension
- acceleration lean
- braking compression
- headlight shutter and lens mechanisms
- boiler, valve, fan, gauge, and exhaust movement
- turret tracking and recoil
- damage vibration, missing-panel motion, and repair recovery
- critical brace
- real-car detachment recoil and independent separated-car motion

### Detachment sequence

The current generic rectangle is replaced by the real rear-car assembly.

The sequence contains:

1. coupling tension
2. confirmation lock
3. coupling fracture
4. locomotive and consist recoil
5. debris and sparks tied to the coupling material
6. real crew/protection state
7. separated car drift using its current damage and upgrade art
8. a persistent gap and changed consist silhouette

Reduced Motion keeps the break and final separation but removes long drift and
camera impulse.

### Focus sequence

Focus contains:

1. headlight housing and shutter response
2. visible energy compression
3. beam narrowing
4. threat or ward material exposure
5. contact reaction
6. exhausted release and recovery

The text label reinforces the action but no longer carries the visual event.

## Risk register and tripwires

| Risk | Tripwire | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Generic steampunk result | Critics compare the frame to current train-defense games rather than naming its headlight/night/sacrifice identity | Redesign silhouette, palette balance, and composition before adding assets |
| Style drift | Subject contact sheets show different cameras, outlines, materials, or light ratios | Rebuild from the style master; no hand patch |
| Over-modeling | Detail disappears at 844 x 390 or breaks tri/texture budgets | Remove geometry and enlarge readable forms |
| Atlas explosion | M3/M4 PCK or texture budgets fail | Split static parts from deformation; remove invisible passes; reduce frame sampling |
| Rigid animation | Movement translates without pose change or recovery | Stop asset scale-out and repair the state rig |
| Particle substitution | More emitters are added without clearer contact or consequence | Replace with authored shapes, reaction, and aftermath |
| Runtime-3D creep | A non-approved gameplay object requests live 3D | Reject it or seek a new decision record with benchmark evidence |
| Mobile unreadability | Train or threat becomes icon-sized behind the HUD | Change camera/world composition; never shrink controls below M1 gates |
| Weak upgrade ownership | Critics cannot identify the advanced variant | Remodel the attachment or silhouette; recolor is insufficient |
| Detachment remains abstract | The separated object is not the real rear car with its state | Block M3B/M4 completion |
| Boss scope explosion | A fourth phase or new rendering pipeline is proposed | Remove or replace an existing beat; no new phase |
| Marketing built late | M3B has no usable screenshots, loop, trailer opening, or capsule | Treat M3B as failed |
| Accessibility added late | Default effect exists without RM/RF/HC behavior | Asset cannot enter production |
| Web-only regression | Effect works only in native or depends on unsupported Compatibility behavior | Replace or provide a feature-parity Web path |
| Self-review blind spot | Independent critics disagree by more than 1.5 points | Run an ambiguity audit and revise the artifact |
| Profitability assumption | Visual score is used as proof of market demand | Require demo and unfamiliar-player evidence before paid-launch recommendation |

## Stop, revise, and go rules

### Stop

Stop scale-out when:

- M3B is below 8.0
- any critical M3B category is below 8.0
- three correction loops do not converge
- the asset factory cannot reproduce accepted outputs
- 844 x 390 cannot remain attractive without violating controls
- the main visual pitch still reads as generic train defense

### Revise

Revise the smallest responsible layer:

1. composition and camera
2. silhouette and scale
3. motion timing and state
4. material and lighting
5. effect shape and aftermath
6. atlas/pass density
7. live-3D use

Do not respond first by adding content, particles, post-processing, or runtime
3D.

### Go

Proceed to public v0.8 release consideration only when:

- final weighted score >=8.0
- internal target >=8.3
- all critical categories >=8.0
- every other category >=7.0
- all technical and accessibility gates pass
- storefront assets honestly match gameplay
- mobile and desktop public frames are both desirable
- vector and baked fallbacks preserve gameplay information
- no known high-confidence blocker remains

## Definition of done

This plan is complete only when:

- the proof slice passed before asset scale-out
- the locomotive looks and moves like the hero character
- all six cars and twelve upgrades visibly change the train
- every regular threat has authored locomotion, anticipation, contact,
  reaction, and death
- Focus and detachment are signature visual moments
- Longshadow has three unmistakable phase silhouettes and transformations
- all twelve signature loops exist and pass default and accessibility review
- environments support the journey fantasy without obscuring play
- UI art belongs to the train while preserving current clarity
- 844 x 390 is a marketing-quality frame, not merely a functional layout
- accessibility variants are authored, not stripped
- all generated assets are reproducible and traceable
- existing performance, package, deterministic, and fallback gates pass
- the complete store asset and trailer set is generated from final gameplay
- three integrated correction rounds close
- the final score reaches the strict release threshold
