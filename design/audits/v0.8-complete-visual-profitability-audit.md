# v0.8 Complete Visual and Commercial-Presentation Audit

Date: September 23, 2026

Baseline: `57bae13` (`Implement v0.8 mobile platform foundation`)

Verdict: **NO-GO for a paid commercial launch; GO for the planned visual
production milestones, with a commercial proof slice added before full asset
scale-out.**

## Executive assessment

The Lantern Line is a strong game prototype with unusually good technical,
accessibility, mobile-layout, performance, and fallback foundations. Its visual
identity is also promising: a fortress train crossing an impossible night is a
clear, marketable fantasy.

The current build does not yet look like a finished paid game. It looks like a
well-designed strategy prototype rendered with coherent programmer art. The
gap is not a lack of visual features or a need to convert the whole game to
runtime 3D. The gap is authored production art, stateful animation, material
response, visual progression, and memorable audiovisual payoffs.

The most important commercial decision is therefore:

> Stop adding breadth until one complete, trailer-ready combat slice proves
> the final art, animation, effects, readability, and mobile composition.

The existing M2-M7 roadmap is directionally correct. It should be tightened so
M3 proves a complete marketable moment before M4 produces all cars, upgrades,
crew, and world kits.

## Audit method

This review covered:

- every committed title, gameplay, route, station, combat, boss, mobile, sprite,
  hybrid, and runtime-3D receipt
- a fresh live Web play sequence at 1280 x 720
- half-second title samples
- one-second early-combat samples
- 100 ms Focus samples
- 150 ms detachment samples
- the complete production drawing and motion code for the world, train,
  enemies, Longshadow, effects, UI transitions, and architecture benchmark
- the v0.8 asset inventory and deterministic art pipeline
- current successful tower-defense and adjacent indie-strategy presentation
  references on Steam
- Steamworks guidance for trailers, screenshots, capsules, and first-impression
  timing

The live motion diagnostic is directional rather than a quality score:

- consecutive title frames changed about 0.74% of screen pixels per 500 ms
- early gameplay changed about 4.02% per second on average
- most gameplay change came from parallax, beam rotation, enemy translation,
  notifications, and resource values rather than authored pose animation

## What already works

### 1. The game has a distinctive fantasy

The combination of a side-view fortress train, headlight aiming, route
projection, car sacrifice, and a supernatural rail journey is substantially
more ownable than a generic tower-defense map.

The title composition already communicates the train, darkness, and headlight.
The brass, ember, coal, bone, and shadow palette supports the premise.

### 2. Information design is stronger than the art finish

Threat roles, target areas, lens recommendations, boss instructions, resource
states, and critical warnings are explicit and accessible. The game remains
playable when art is simple because the information layer is disciplined.

That is a valuable foundation, but the large explanatory labels currently do
too much of the emotional and readability work that animation and form should
eventually carry.

### 3. Mobile and accessibility foundations are commercially valuable

The dedicated touch HUD, safe areas, physical targets, scalable controls,
High Contrast, Reduced Motion, Reduced Flashes, and input cancellation are
well ahead of the current art maturity.

These systems should constrain the new art rather than be rewritten around it.

### 4. The renderer decision is correct

The existing evidence supports layered baked sprites for normal gameplay and
bounded live 3D only where it creates obvious presentation value. Full runtime
3D nearly triples representative draw calls while the current runtime-3D
receipt does not look better enough to justify that cost.

The profitable choice is not "more 3D." It is better-authored 3D sources,
better baked outputs, stronger rigs, and selective live 3D for moments players
will remember or share.

## Critical commercial blockers

### 1. The production visual asset phase has not started

The M0 asset receipt still reports:

- categories: none
- generated artifacts: 0
- no controlled scene-first `.blend` sources

The shipping world, train, threats, boss, and effects remain the v0.7 vector
renderers. The v0.8 benchmark modes are architecture proxies, not candidate
production art.

This is the largest gap. No amount of UI polish, camera shake, glow, or
additional particles can make primitive benchmark geometry and immediate-mode
drawings look like authored final assets.

### 2. The train has insufficient mass and mechanical life

Current motion in `TrainRenderer` consists mainly of:

- rotating wheel spokes
- generated smoke circles
- short wheel sparks
- damage outline pulses

The locomotive body, cab, boiler, suspension, couplings, cars, mounted
equipment, and crew do not visibly react as a connected machine.

Needed:

- heavy suspension cycle with phase-offset cars
- wheel and rod motion tied to speed
- boiler, exhaust, valve, fan, gauge, shutter, and coupling motion
- acceleration lean, braking compression, hit recoil, cannon recoil, and
  detachment recoil
- powered, unpowered, damaged, critical, and repaired motion states
- visible crew work and brace states

The train is the product's hero character. It needs more animation attention
than any individual enemy.

### 3. Threat movement communicates position, not behavior

`EnemyDirector._update_enemy()` moves each enemy directly toward a target.
The drawing code then renders mostly fixed body geometry at that position.

Current exceptions are limited:

- Drainer tendrils sway
- wards rotate
- attack-warning arcs expand

Missing:

- locomotion cycles
- acceleration and skid
- climb and latch
- attack wind-up
- held anticipation pose
- attack contact
- target reaction
- stagger
- death or escape

The result is readable but emotionally flat. Enemies appear to slide rather
than hunt, climb, feed, bite, or strike.

### 4. Effects confirm events but do not make them satisfying

The effects layer currently provides:

- straight tracers
- expanding rings and spokes
- three-line shadow slashes
- broken ward arcs
- full-screen color flashes
- random screen shake
- a generic detached-car rectangle

The Focus sequence is primarily a narrower beam and the text `FOCUS BEAM`.
It has no clear charge, compression, contact buildup, enemy reaction, or
release punctuation.

The detachment sequence is strategically strong but visually weak. The rear
car disappears from the train, a generic rectangular proxy begins drifting
left and down, and a message explains the sacrifice. It lacks:

- coupling tension before release
- a visible coupling snap
- train recoil and car suspension response
- sparks, steam, debris, and torn cable
- crew consequence staging
- a brief camera composition that preserves control
- a distinct silhouette and damage state for the actual detached car

Detachment should be one of the game's signature trailer moments. It currently
looks like a state change with a placeholder after-image.

### 5. Longshadow is mechanically clear but not visually climactic

The three phases use different geometric bodies, which is a good beginning.
The current boss presentation is still made from polygons, arcs, a pulse, a
vertical bob, and phase-specific lines.

Needed:

- a larger and more imposing screen-space composition
- phase transformations that visibly tear, fold, or reform negative space
- tendril animation with authored intent
- entrance anticipation before the body resolves
- active-response hit reactions
- attack recovery and stagger
- environmental corruption that affects nearby light, rails, ash, and
  silhouettes
- a defeat sequence with a strong visual release into dawn

The boss should provide the screenshots and short clips that distinguish the
game from every normal train-defense encounter.

### 6. Environment layers are too abstract and repetitive

The current environment uses gradients, sinusoidal ridges, signal towers,
simple route landmarks, ash lines, rain lines, and moving ties.

It successfully avoids gameplay obstruction, but it does not yet create a
sense of place, journey, or discovery.

Needed:

- four authored depth bands with different edge treatment and atmospheric
  contrast
- route-specific landmark kits with large recognizable forms
- foreground objects that cross the frame without obscuring targets
- light interaction on fog, rain, glass, metal, bone, vegetation, and water
- progression in weather, density, damage, and dawn color over the run
- occasional large compositional events that make route choices visually
  memorable

### 7. Upgrades do not yet create enough visible ownership

Current cars have useful shape differences and some upgrade geometry, but
upgrades are still often communicated by tiny attachments and an `UP` badge.

A profitable upgrade game needs players to want screenshots of their machine.
Every meaningful upgrade should visibly change one or more of:

- silhouette
- animation
- lighting
- weapon behavior
- crew activity
- damage tolerance
- ambient effect

The player should be able to compare an early and late train without reading
the HUD and immediately understand that the machine became more powerful.

### 8. UI is effective but visually resembles a debug/control surface

Desktop panels consume a large part of the frame, and the mobile layout
correctly reserves space but makes the current train and enemies comparatively
small. Most controls are rectangular text buttons with limited visual state.

Needed:

- one coherent icon family
- stronger selected, pressed, charging, cooldown, disabled, and warning states
- resource changes that animate locally instead of relying on text values
- cooldown rings and hold progress attached to action affordances
- more compact desktop information grouping
- diegetic instrument and signal language without sacrificing accessibility
- modal art that reflects a route projection table, workshop, crew roster, and
  ledger rather than a generic dark panel

The HUD should frame the train fantasy, not look like a separate administration
application laid over it.

### 9. The current first ten seconds do not sell the unique action

Steamworks states that players may give a game less than ten seconds in a
Discovery Queue and recommends putting real gameplay first, with the actual HUD
visible.

The current title has atmosphere, but the first live combat frames primarily
show:

- static vector train
- large HUD panels
- moving light polygons
- enemies entering from the right
- explanatory banners

The game needs a mute-readable opening clip that shows the complete promise:

1. the headlight reveals a distinct threat
2. the threat performs a clear wind-up
3. the train responds with a visible weapon or Focus action
4. the hit has material-specific impact and reaction
5. an upgrade, route projection, detachment, or boss image establishes the
   strategic hook

## 3D-specific audit

### Current state

There are no production 3D visual assets in the shipping game.

The runtime-3D benchmark builds boxes, cylinders, spheres, simple materials,
lights, and an orthographic camera in code. It proves:

- WebGL 2 compatibility
- performance measurement
- SubViewport composition
- material and light plumbing
- a safe boundary for selective live 3D

It does not prove:

- final modeling quality
- UV or texture quality
- rig quality
- animation quality
- baked-sprite quality
- final readability at gameplay scale
- artistic consistency with Coal-lit Ink & Brass

### Required improvement

Use 3D as an authorship and rendering pipeline:

- script-built modular Blender sources
- exaggerated silhouette-first proportions
- shared orthographic camera and lighting
- broad material groups rather than tiny PBR detail
- layered outputs for body, moving parts, light, shadow, normal, and masks
- consistent pivots and sockets
- clean High and Low atlases

Use live 3D only for approved High-profile landmarks, transitions, and
Longshadow moments where depth, lighting, or camera movement is visibly worth
the cost.

Do not spend production time converting ordinary train cars and regular
threats to full runtime 3D gameplay.

## Market comparison and profitability implications

Current successful strategy and tower-defense games show that commercial
success does not require one specific rendering style:

- Thronefall succeeds with minimalist stylized 3D.
- Dome Keeper succeeds with pixel art.
- Kingdom Rush succeeds with hand-authored cartoon 2D.
- Bloons TD 6 succeeds with highly readable cartoony 3D.
- Bad North succeeds with restrained miniature-diorama 3D.
- Monster Train 2 succeeds with rich illustrated 2D, animation, effects, and
  train-themed spectacle.

The common qualities are:

- instantly recognizable silhouettes
- a coherent art language
- readable action at a glance
- strong attack anticipation and contact
- visible progression and ownership
- effects that communicate both mechanics and material
- screenshots that contain a clear focal event
- a trailer that explains the game without depending on audio or paragraphs

The Lantern Line does not need to match the raw content volume of the largest
franchises. It does need to match their clarity, intentionality, animation
timing, and screenshot quality within its chosen scope.

## Revised production priority

### Priority 0: M2 rendering separation

Complete the planned view/event/fallback separation first. The current
simulation and immediate-mode visuals are too coupled for production rigs and
pooled effects.

Do not compromise this milestone to get attractive screenshots faster.

### Priority 1: one commercial proof slice

Before building every asset family, produce one complete final-quality slice:

- hero locomotive
- one highly readable car
- one visible upgrade transformation
- one Pursuer with full locomotion, anticipation, impact, stagger, and death
- one Defense weapon and one Focus sequence
- one route-specific environment
- one critical-damage state
- one detachment sequence using the real car art
- final HUD art for only the controls visible in that slice
- default, High Contrast, Reduced Motion, Reduced Flashes, and 844 x 390
  variants

The slice must yield:

- one strong title frame
- five store-quality gameplay screenshots
- one six-second loop
- one ten-second gameplay-first trailer opening
- one mobile screenshot that still makes the train and threat look desirable

If this slice does not look commercially convincing, revise the art direction,
camera, scale, materials, animation, and effects before M4 scales production.

### Priority 2: train ownership and world variety

After the proof slice passes:

- complete all car families and upgrade silhouettes
- add crew rigs and powered states
- build route biome and landmark kits
- implement damage, repair, critical, destroyed, and detachment states
- make early, middle, and late trains visually distinct

### Priority 3: combat feel and enemy personality

Then complete:

- enemy state rigs
- weapon rigs
- attack anticipation
- impact and reaction
- ward shatter
- repair and defense effects
- pooled debris and particles
- material response matrix

Every combat event should follow:

1. anticipation
2. contact
3. reaction
4. recovery

### Priority 4: Longshadow spectacle

Build the boss only after the train, one threat, and the core effect language
are proven. Longshadow should reuse the same material, timing, accessibility,
and fallback systems rather than inventing a second pipeline.

### Priority 5: final UI and store presentation

Finish iconography, panel art, cooldowns, selection motion, resource feedback,
title composition, ledgers, and marketing captures after the world art is
stable.

## Commercial quality gates to add

Add these gates to M3-M7:

### Thumbnail gate

At a 320 px-wide capture:

- the locomotive remains the focal object
- the threat class is identifiable
- the player action is visible
- the screenshot has one obvious story

### No-text gate

With tutorial banners and nonessential labels hidden:

- the train state remains readable
- the enemy role remains readable
- attack anticipation remains readable
- impact and result remain readable

### Six-second loop gate

Each signature action must produce a loop with:

- a recognizable starting state
- anticipation
- contact
- visible consequence
- a stable final read

Required loops:

- Focus
- standard defense fire
- heavy cannon
- flak
- ward shatter
- repair
- detachment
- each Longshadow phase transition

### Upgrade ownership gate

An unfamiliar reviewer shown two train screenshots must correctly identify the
more advanced train without reading UI text.

### Mobile desire gate

At 844 x 390:

- the train is not reduced to a thin icon strip
- enemies retain pose readability
- effects do not disappear behind controls
- one screenshot is attractive enough to use publicly

### Storefront gate

Before a paid release:

- first trailer is gameplay-first
- the unique interaction is understandable without sound in ten seconds
- screenshots are captured at 1920 x 1080 or larger
- at least five screenshots show different decisions or signature events
- capsule art uses a finished locomotive and Longshadow composition, not UI
- no public marketing image contains benchmark proxies or temporary vector art

## What not to do

- Do not turn the complete game into runtime 3D.
- Do not model all six cars before the proof slice passes.
- Do not add more particles without improving timing, shape, and reaction.
- Do not rely on full-screen flash and shake as substitutes for impact.
- Do not keep adding explanatory text where pose and animation should teach.
- Do not shrink final art to fit the current desktop HUD without revisiting
  composition.
- Do not market the current benchmark or vector screenshots as near-final art.
- Do not weaken the accessibility variants to gain visual intensity.

## Final recommendation

The game should continue, but it should not be sold on its current visuals.

Its profitable presentation path is:

1. preserve the excellent technical, mobile, and accessibility foundation
2. complete M2
3. create one uncompromising commercial proof slice
4. audit that slice at desktop, mobile, accessibility, thumbnail, and
   trailer scale
5. revise until it looks like a product rather than a prototype
6. only then scale the art across cars, threats, world kits, Longshadow, and UI

The underlying concept is strong enough to justify the investment. The next
commercial breakthrough will come from execution quality, not additional game
systems.
